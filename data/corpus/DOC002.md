# The Union and its Territory (Articles 1-4)

Part I of the Constitution, Articles 1 to 4, deals with the Union and its territory. Article 1 states that India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States. The phrase Union of States was chosen because the Indian federation is not the result of an agreement between states and no state has the right to secede from it. The territory of India comprises the territories of the States, the Union territories and any other territories that may be acquired. Under Article 3, Parliament may by law form a new State, increase or diminish the area of a State, alter the boundaries of a State or change its name. A Bill for this purpose can be introduced only on the recommendation of the President, and the President must refer it to the concerned State Legislature for its views, although Parliament is not bound by those views. Article 2 allows Parliament to admit or establish new States, and Article 4 clarifies that such laws are not constitutional amendments under Article 368.
