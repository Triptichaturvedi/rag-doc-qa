# Right to Constitutional Remedies and Writs (Articles 32 and 226)

Article 32 gives every person the right to move the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights, and Dr B. R. Ambedkar described it as the heart and soul of the Constitution. The Supreme Court has the power to issue directions, orders or writs, including five kinds of writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. Habeas corpus requires a detained person to be produced before the court, mandamus commands a public authority to perform its legal duty, prohibition stops a lower court from exceeding its jurisdiction, certiorari quashes an order made without jurisdiction, and quo warranto questions a person's right to hold a public office. Article 226 gives the High Courts the power to issue writs not only for Fundamental Rights but also for any other purpose, so the writ jurisdiction of a High Court is wider than that of the Supreme Court under Article 32. Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right and cannot be suspended except during an Emergency as provided in the Constitution.
