# Centre-State Relations and the Seventh Schedule (Articles 245-263)

Part XI of the Constitution distributes legislative powers between the Union and the States. Article 246 divides subjects into three lists in the Seventh Schedule. The Union List contains subjects on which only Parliament may legislate, such as defence, foreign affairs and railways. The State List contains subjects such as police, public order, public health and agriculture, on which State Legislatures legislate. The Concurrent List contains subjects such as education, forests, marriage and criminal law, on which both can legislate, and if a State law conflicts with a Union law on a Concurrent subject the Union law prevails under Article 254. Under Article 248 the residuary power to legislate on subjects not mentioned in any list belongs to Parliament. Article 249 allows Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in the national interest if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution supported by two-thirds of the members present and voting. Article 263 provides for an Inter-State Council, and the 101st Amendment in 2016 introduced the Goods and Services Tax and the GST Council under Article 279A.
