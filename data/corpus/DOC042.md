# NASA Systems Engineering Handbook: Project Life Cycle

The NASA Systems Engineering Handbook describes the processes used to engineer complex space systems. The NASA project life cycle is divided into two major stages, Formulation and Implementation, which are further divided into phases. Formulation includes Pre-Phase A (concept studies), Phase A (concept and technology development) and Phase B (preliminary design and technology completion). Implementation includes Phase C (final design and fabrication), Phase D (system assembly, integration and test, and launch), Phase E (operations and sustainment) and Phase F (closeout). Key Decision Points, labelled KDP A to KDP F, are reviews where the decision authority decides whether a project may proceed to the next phase. Technical reviews occur along the way, such as the System Requirements Review, the Preliminary Design Review at the end of Phase B and the Critical Design Review in Phase C. The handbook describes 17 common technical processes in three groups: system design, product realisation and technical management. Verification confirms that a product meets its requirements, while validation confirms that it fulfils the intended use in the operational environment.
