# OSHA Hazard Communication Standard and GHS Labels (29 CFR 1910.1200)

The OSHA Hazard Communication Standard, found in 29 CFR 1910.1200, requires that information about chemical hazards is passed to workers through labels, safety data sheets and training. Since 2012 it has been aligned with the Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals, known as GHS. A shipped container label must carry six elements: a product identifier, a signal word, hazard statements, precautionary statements, pictograms and the supplier identification. The signal word is Danger for the more severe hazards and Warning for the less severe hazards. OSHA requires eight of the nine GHS pictograms, such as the flame, skull and crossbones, and corrosion symbols, while the environmental pictogram is not mandatory. A safety data sheet has a standard 16-section format, including identification, hazard identification, composition, first-aid measures, fire-fighting measures, handling and storage, exposure controls and personal protection, physical and chemical properties, and disposal considerations. Employers must keep safety data sheets accessible to workers during each shift, maintain a written hazard communication programme, and train workers on the labels and the hazards in their work area.
