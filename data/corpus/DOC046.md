# NOAA Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale

The Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale used by the US National Hurricane Center rates hurricanes in categories 1 to 5 according to their maximum sustained wind speed, defined as a one-minute average. A tropical depression has winds up to 38 miles per hour, a tropical storm has winds of 39 to 73 miles per hour, and a hurricane has sustained winds of 74 miles per hour or more. Category 1 has winds of 74 to 95 miles per hour and causes some damage such as to power lines and roofs. Category 2 has winds of 96 to 110 miles per hour and causes extensive damage. Category 3 has winds of 111 to 129 miles per hour, Category 4 has 130 to 156 miles per hour, and Category 5 has winds of 157 miles per hour or higher. Categories 3, 4 and 5 are called major hurricanes because of their potential for significant loss of life and damage. The scale considers wind only and does not account for storm surge, rainfall flooding or tornadoes, which can cause more fatalities than wind. Other ocean basins use different scales, and in the North Indian Ocean the India Meteorological Department uses its own cyclone classification.
