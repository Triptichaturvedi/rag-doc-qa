# The President of India (Articles 52-62)

The Constitution provides for a President of India under Article 52, and Article 53 vests the executive power of the Union in the President, to be exercised directly or through officers subordinate to the President. The President is elected under Article 54 by an electoral college consisting of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States, including the National Capital Territory of Delhi and Puducherry. The election follows the system of proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, and the voting is by secret ballot. The President holds office for five years and is eligible for re-election. To be a candidate a person must be a citizen of India, must have completed thirty-five years of age and must be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha. Under Article 61 the President can be removed by impeachment for violation of the Constitution, which requires a resolution passed by a majority of not less than two-thirds of the total membership of each House. The President also has the power to grant pardons under Article 72 and to promulgate ordinances under Article 123 when Parliament is not in session.
