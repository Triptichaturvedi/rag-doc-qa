# NIST SP 800-61 Computer Security Incident Handling Guide

NIST Special Publication 800-61 gives guidance on preparing for, detecting, analysing and responding to computer security incidents. Revision 2 describes an incident response life cycle of four phases: Preparation; Detection and Analysis; Containment, Eradication and Recovery; and Post-Incident Activity. In the preparation phase an organisation creates an incident response policy and plan, forms a response team, and acquires tools and training. Detection and analysis relies on precursors and indicators from sources such as intrusion detection systems, logs and user reports, and the team documents, prioritises and notifies stakeholders. Containment limits the damage, for example by isolating an infected host, eradication removes the cause such as malware, and recovery restores systems to normal operation and monitors them. After an incident a lessons-learned meeting is held to improve processes. Revision 3, published in 2025, realigns incident response with the six functions of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 and treats response as part of overall risk management rather than a separate cycle. Evidence handling and chain of custody are important when legal action is possible.
