# Parliament of India: Composition and Terms (Articles 79-122)

Under Article 79, Parliament consists of the President and two Houses, the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and the House of the People (Lok Sabha). The Rajya Sabha has a maximum strength of 250 members, of whom 238 represent the States and Union territories and 12 are nominated by the President for special knowledge in literature, science, art and social service. It is a permanent House that is not subject to dissolution, and one-third of its members retire every second year, each member serving a term of six years. The Lok Sabha represents the people directly and has a maximum strength of 550 members, with 543 elected members at present. Its normal term is five years unless it is dissolved earlier. A candidate must be at least 25 years old for the Lok Sabha and 30 years old for the Rajya Sabha. The voting age was reduced from 21 to 18 years by the 61st Amendment in 1988. Under Article 85 not more than six months may intervene between two sessions of Parliament.
