# Fundamental Rights: Overview and Right to Equality (Articles 12-18)

Part III of the Constitution (Articles 12 to 35) guarantees the Fundamental Rights, which are enforceable by the courts. Article 12 defines the State, and Article 13 declares that laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are void to the extent of the inconsistency. Originally seven in number, the Fundamental Rights are now six groups because the right to property was removed from Part III by the 44th Amendment in 1978 and became a legal right under Article 300A. The Right to Equality covers Articles 14 to 18. Article 14 guarantees equality before the law and the equal protection of the laws. Article 15 prohibits discrimination only on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. Article 16 ensures equality of opportunity in public employment. Article 17 abolishes untouchability and makes its practice an offence, and Article 18 abolishes titles other than military and academic distinctions.
