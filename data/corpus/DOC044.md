# US Army Map Reading and Land Navigation (FM 3-25.26)

The US Army field manual on map reading and land navigation teaches soldiers how to use maps and a compass. Marginal information on a map sheet gives the map name, sheet number, scale, contour interval, legend and declination diagram. Locations are given using grid coordinates. A four-digit grid coordinate locates a point to within 1,000 metres, a six-digit grid coordinate locates a point to within 100 metres, and an eight-digit grid coordinate locates a point to within 10 metres. Coordinates are read by giving the easting first and then the northing, a rule remembered as read right, then up. A navigator deals with three norths: true north toward the North Pole, grid north defined by the vertical grid lines, and magnetic north toward which a compass needle points. The declination diagram shows the angles between them. An azimuth is the horizontal angle measured clockwise from a north base line, from 0 to 360 degrees, and a back azimuth is found by adding 180 degrees to an azimuth below 180 or subtracting 180 degrees from one above 180. Contour lines connect points of equal elevation, and every fifth contour line is a heavier index contour labelled with its elevation.
