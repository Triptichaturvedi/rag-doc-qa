# USDA Safe Minimum Internal Temperatures for Food

The US Department of Agriculture advises that a food thermometer is the only reliable way to know whether food has reached a safe internal temperature that kills harmful bacteria. The safe minimum internal temperature for whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb is 145 degrees Fahrenheit (62.8 degrees Celsius), measured with a food thermometer, followed by a three-minute rest time. Ground meats such as beef, pork, veal and lamb must reach 160 degrees Fahrenheit (71.1 degrees Celsius). All poultry, including ground poultry, must reach 165 degrees Fahrenheit (73.9 degrees Celsius), and so must leftovers and casseroles when reheated. Fish and shellfish should be cooked to 145 degrees Fahrenheit, and egg dishes to 160 degrees Fahrenheit. Bacteria multiply fastest in the danger zone between 40 and 140 degrees Fahrenheit (4.4 and 60 degrees Celsius), so perishable food should not be left out for more than two hours, or one hour when the outdoor temperature is above 90 degrees Fahrenheit. The thermometer should be inserted into the thickest part of the food. This summary is general food safety information and not medical advice.
