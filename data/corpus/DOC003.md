# Citizenship (Articles 5-11)

Part II of the Constitution, Articles 5 to 11, deals with citizenship. Article 5 declared who were citizens at the commencement of the Constitution: persons domiciled in India who were born in India, or whose parent was born in India, or who had been ordinarily resident in India for at least five years. Articles 6 and 7 dealt with migrants from Pakistan, and Article 8 with persons of Indian origin residing abroad. Article 9 provides that a person who voluntarily acquires the citizenship of a foreign State ceases to be a citizen of India. Article 11 empowers Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law, and Parliament exercised this power by enacting the Citizenship Act, 1955, which lays down how citizenship may be acquired: by birth, by descent, by registration, by naturalisation and by incorporation of territory. India follows a system of single citizenship, so there is no separate state citizenship, and the Overseas Citizen of India card is not dual citizenship.
