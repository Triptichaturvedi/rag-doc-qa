# Emergency Provisions (Articles 352-360)

Part XVIII of the Constitution contains the emergency provisions. Under Article 352 the President can proclaim a National Emergency when the security of India is threatened by war, external aggression or armed rebellion. The 44th Amendment in 1978 replaced the words internal disturbance with armed rebellion and required that the President act only on the written recommendation of the Union Cabinet. The proclamation must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within one month by a special majority, and it continues for six months unless renewed. Article 356 deals with failure of constitutional machinery in a State, known as President's Rule, which must be approved by Parliament within two months and may last up to three years in total. In S. R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994) the Supreme Court held that a proclamation under Article 356 is subject to judicial review. Article 360 provides for a Financial Emergency. During a National Emergency the rights under Article 19 are suspended, but the 44th Amendment provides that the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21 and protection in respect of conviction under Article 20 cannot be suspended.
