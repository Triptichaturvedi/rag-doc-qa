# State Executive, Legislature and High Courts (Articles 153-237)

Each State has a Governor under Article 153, who is appointed by the President under Article 155 and holds office during the pleasure of the President, usually for a term of five years. The Governor must be a citizen of India aged at least thirty-five years. The executive power of the State is exercised by the Governor with the aid and advice of a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister under Article 163. The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor and the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly. The Legislative Assembly of a State has not more than 500 and not less than 60 members, and its normal term is five years. Some States also have a Legislative Council. Every State has a High Court under Article 214, and Parliament may establish a common High Court for two or more States. High Court judges retire at the age of 62 under Article 217. Under Article 227 a High Court exercises superintendence over all courts and tribunals within its territorial jurisdiction.
