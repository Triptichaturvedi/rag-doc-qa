# Legislative Procedure, Money Bills and the Budget (Articles 107-122, 265-267)

An ordinary Bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament and must be passed by both Houses before it receives the assent of the President. If the Houses disagree, the President may summon a joint sitting under Article 108, which is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha. A Money Bill, defined in Article 110, can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha on the recommendation of the President. The Rajya Sabha may only make recommendations on a Money Bill and must return it within fourteen days, and the Lok Sabha may accept or reject those recommendations. The Speaker's decision whether a Bill is a Money Bill is final. Article 112 requires the President to lay an annual financial statement, known as the Budget, before Parliament each year. Article 265 provides that no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law. All revenues received by the Government of India are credited to the Consolidated Fund of India under Article 266, and Article 267 creates the Contingency Fund of India for unforeseen expenditure.
