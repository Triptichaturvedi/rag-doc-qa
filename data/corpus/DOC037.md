# NIST SP 800-63B Digital Identity Guidelines: Authentication

NIST Special Publication 800-63B sets technical requirements for authentication and the management of authenticators such as passwords, one-time codes, hardware tokens and passkeys. Revision 4, finalised in 2025, requires verifiers to demand a minimum password length of 15 characters when a password is the only authentication factor, and a minimum of 8 characters when the password is used only as part of multi-factor authentication. Verifiers should permit a maximum length of at least 64 characters so that passphrases can be used. The guidelines forbid composition rules that force a mix of character types, and they say organisations should not require users to change passwords periodically unless there is evidence of compromise. New passwords should be checked against a blocklist of commonly used or compromised passwords. Authenticator Assurance Levels AAL1, AAL2 and AAL3 describe increasing confidence in authentication: AAL2 requires two distinct factors and AAL3 requires a hardware-based cryptographic authenticator that resists phishing. Passwords must be stored using a salted and hashed approach with a suitable key-derivation function. Earlier revisions required only 8 characters, so older internal policies may be out of date.
