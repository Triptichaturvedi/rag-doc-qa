# FEMA Emergency Preparedness: Get Informed, Make a Plan, Build a Kit

Federal Emergency Management Agency preparedness guidance, published through Ready.gov, tells households to prepare for emergencies in three steps: get informed about hazards in the area, make a family emergency plan, and build an emergency supply kit. A family plan should identify how members will contact one another, where they will meet if they cannot go home, and the evacuation routes and shelters nearby, and it should include plans for children, older adults, people with disabilities and pets. The basic supply kit should keep enough supplies for at least three days. Recommended items include one gallon of water per person per day for at least three days, a three-day supply of non-perishable food, a battery-powered or hand-crank radio, a flashlight, a first aid kit, extra batteries, a whistle to signal for help, dust masks, plastic sheeting and duct tape for sheltering in place, moist towelettes, garbage bags, a wrench or pliers to turn off utilities, a manual can opener, local maps and a mobile phone with chargers and a backup battery. Households should also keep copies of important documents and review the kit every year.
