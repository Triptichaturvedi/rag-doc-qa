# USGS Topographic Maps: Scale, Contours and Symbols

The US Geological Survey produces topographic maps that show the shape of the land surface and features such as water, roads and buildings. The classic series covers a quadrangle of 7.5 minutes of latitude and longitude at a scale of 1:24,000, where one inch on the map represents 2,000 feet on the ground. Contour lines connect points of equal elevation above mean sea level, and the contour interval is the vertical distance between adjacent lines. Closely spaced contours indicate a steep slope, widely spaced contours indicate a gentle slope, and contours that form closed circles show hills or depressions, with small tick marks called hachures indicating a depression. Colours carry meaning: brown for contours, blue for water, green for vegetation, black for cultural features such as buildings and most roads, red for major roads and public land survey lines, and purple for revisions made from aerial photographs. A bar scale on the map allows distances to be measured. Digital US Topo maps replaced the paper series and can be downloaded free of charge from the USGS.
