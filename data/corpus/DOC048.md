# OSHA Lockout/Tagout: Control of Hazardous Energy (29 CFR 1910.147)

The OSHA standard on the control of hazardous energy, commonly called lockout/tagout and found in 29 CFR 1910.147, protects workers who service or maintain machines from the unexpected energisation or start-up of the machine and from the release of stored energy. Employers must establish an energy control programme with written procedures, employee training and periodic inspections at least annually. A typical lockout procedure has six steps. First, prepare for shutdown by identifying the type and magnitude of the energy. Second, shut down the machine using normal stopping procedures. Third, isolate the machine from all energy sources by operating switches, valves or disconnects. Fourth, apply lockout or tagout devices to the isolating points. Fifth, release or restrain all stored or residual energy such as that in springs, capacitors and hydraulic lines. Sixth, verify isolation by attempting to start the machine, after making sure that no one is exposed. Each authorised employee applies a personal lock and keeps the key, and only the person who applied a lock may remove it. Training is required for authorised employees, affected employees and other employees in the area.
