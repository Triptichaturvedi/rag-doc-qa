# Directive Principles of State Policy (Articles 36-51)

Part IV of the Constitution, Articles 36 to 51, contains the Directive Principles of State Policy. Article 37 states that these principles are not enforceable by any court but are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country, and the State has a duty to apply them in making laws. They aim at a welfare state and social and economic democracy. Article 39 directs the State towards adequate means of livelihood, fair distribution of material resources and equal pay for equal work for men and women. Article 40 directs the State to organise village panchayats, Article 44 to secure a uniform civil code for citizens, Article 45 to provide early childhood care and education for children below six years, Article 48A to protect and improve the environment, and Article 50 to separate the judiciary from the executive. Article 51 promotes international peace and security. In Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980) the Supreme Court held that harmony and balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles is part of the basic structure of the Constitution.
